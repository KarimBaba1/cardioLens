    How the approaches compare

    Every approach was scored on the same patients from the held-out test set — patients that no model saw during training or tuning. Thresholds were chosen on a separate validation set.

    Why the app uses Logistic Regression
    • Highest ROC-AUC (ability to rank higher-risk patients above lower-risk ones), tied with the alternatives.
    • Simplest model, trains in seconds, and gives an exact explanation for each patient.
    • The fine-tuned LLM only matched it after ~100 minutes of GPU training; the zero-shot LLM was clearly weaker.
    • The differences between the top four are small relative to the test sample (few positive cases), so no approach is clearly superior.

    Performance across patient groups

    An overall score can hide weaker performance for some groups.

    Groups with fewer than 20 positive cases are marked limited data; their results are unreliable. Differences between groups show where the model may be less reliable — they do not establish that it is clinically fair or safe.

    Metrics explained

    Recall
    Of the patients who had a CHD event, the share the model flagged.
    Precision
    Of the patients the model flagged, the share who had a CHD event.
    F1-score
    Balance of precision and recall at the chosen threshold.
    ROC-AUC
    How well risk scores rank patients, independent of threshold. 0.5 = chance, 1.0 = perfect.

    Data & method

    Data. Framingham Heart Study teaching subset (Kaggle, 4,238 people, one row each). Target: TenYearCHD — whether a coronary heart disease event occurred within ten years (15% of people).

    Splits. Stratified 70 / 15 / 15 into training, validation and test sets with a fixed seed. Missing values are filled using training data only.

    Approaches. Logistic Regression, Random Forest, the clinical Framingham Risk Score (BMI version), and the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct language model used zero-shot and after QLoRA fine-tuning.

    Integrity. Test labels never appeared in prompts, training or tuning. Thresholds were chosen on validation data.

    Summary. The plain-language summary is written by Qwen2.5-7B running locally (no data leaves the computer). Its output is checked: if it changes a number or gives treatment advice, a fixed template is shown instead.

    Limitations & responsible use

    • Moderate accuracy: ROC-AUC ≈ 0.69 on unseen patients. Many patients who later had an event are not flagged.
    • Historical US cohort; results may not transfer to other populations, including Hong Kong.
    • The Framingham baseline (BMI version) predicts general cardiovascular disease, broader than the CHD outcome used here.
    • Language-model results use a 300-patient test subset and a 4-bit compressed model; one training run only.
    • Factor influences and what-if results describe the model's associations, not causal effects of treatment or lifestyle change.
    • Group results reveal uneven performance but cannot prove fairness or safety.
    • Inputs are limited to the ranges seen in training (e.g. age 32–70).
    • Designed for people without existing cardiovascular disease: for a patient with a previous stroke the app shows a scope notice instead of a screening result (only 18 such patients in the training data).
    • Correlated inputs share effects: e.g. diabetes adds little when glucose is normal, and a low blood pressure achieved with medication still lowers the estimate. Rare combinations are flagged as less reliable.

    CardioLens is an academic prototype (UFCE3P-30-3, AY26/27). It must not be used with real patients or as a replacement for medical diagnosis or treatment.
